## Official Linux/Python semantics

Linux `flock` is advisory and attached to the open-file description. Duplicated/forked descriptors share ownership; independent opens can conflict even within one process. A stable exclusive nonblocking acquisition rejects a conflicting owner. The lock persists until its descriptor copies close, and survives exec unless descriptor inheritance is prevented. These are narrower than a process-ID lease or global writer registry. [Linux flock manual](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/flock.2.html), [Python 3.14 fcntl](https://docs.python.org/3.14/library/fcntl.html).

The reviewer ran a bounded actual Linux temporary-lock probe (stdout only): a second independent open in the same process was busy; a separate process was busy; close then allowed acquisition. Unlinking/recreating the path allowed a second lock while the original inode remained locked. Consequently normal close must not unlink/replace a coordination file. This is a synthetic OS observation, not production source acceptance; no standalone raw log was saved or invented.

Use close-only rather than LOCK_UN for a shared fork description, O_CLOEXEC/non-inheritable fd for helpers, one EX|NB mode without lock upgrades, and no stale PID/TTL unlock. Unsupported acquisition/ownership errors must remain failures, distinct from busy. None of this prevents uncooperative writers or certifies NFS/SMB/power-loss behavior.
